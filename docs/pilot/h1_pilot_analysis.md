# H1 feasibility pilot — 30 call sites, two repositories, five baselines

Project: Post-quantum crypto scanner for Python projects. Base paper DOI 10.1145/3774748.3795653 (Pillar P1). Date: 12 Nov 2026. Inputs: `h1_pilot_sites.csv` (annotations), `h1_pilot_joined.csv` and `h1_pilot_summary.md` (generated by `experiments/pilot/join_tool_outputs.py`), `raw_tool_outputs/` (unmodified tool outputs), `pilot_manifest.json` (commits, versions, invocations).

## 1. Purpose and pre-stated decision rule

The pilot tests whether the research problem is observable before any analyzer is built: (H1a) is the cryptographic role of RSA/EC call sites often *not* determinable from the method or algorithm name alone, and (H1b) do existing scanners consequently issue role-inappropriate, undecided or false-assurance recommendations? The hostile audit (`docs/research/02_hostile_novelty_audit.md`, Sec. 13) set a provisional kill criterion: redesign if fewer than ~10% of sites are name-undeterminable **and** baselines' harmful + undecided rate is below ~10%. Those thresholds are treated as provisional assumptions; the pre-registered thresholds for the full study are in `docs/specification/evaluation_protocol.md` Sec. 1 (25% and 10%).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 How often is the role undeterminable from the name?

| Quantity | Value (n = 30 eligible sites) |
|---|---|
| Role not determinable from method/algorithm name alone | 16 / 30 = 53% (Wilson 95% CI 36–70%) |
| Statically indeterminate (depends on a runtime value) | 4 / 30 = 13% (CI 5–30%) |
| Needs cross-function evidence (same class/module) | 15 sites; cross-module (runtime): 4 |
| Roles | SIGN 12, VERIFY 5, KEY_ESTABLISHMENT 5, UNKNOWN 4, KEY_MATERIAL_ONLY 2, PK_ENCRYPT 1, PK_DECRYPT 1 |
| Provenance | attribute 13, loaded-PEM 5, generated 4, loaded-DER 2, loaded-JWK 2, parsed-blob 2, loaded-SSH 1, certificate 1 |

All 16 name-undeterminable sites are key-generation, key-loading or key-construction sites; every operation site (`sign`, `verify`, `exchange`, `encrypt`, `decrypt`) was determinable from the method once the receiver is an RSA/EC key. The four indeterminate sites are the four key loaders of python-jose's `CryptographyRSAKey`, whose key serves either JWS verification/signing or JWE key wrap depending on the runtime `algorithm` string.

### 3.2 What do the baselines recommend?

Per-site judgments (n = 30 eligible sites; full matrix in `h1_pilot_summary.md`):

| Tool | CORRECT | INCORRECT (harmful) | UNDECIDED (dual target) | DETECTED, no target | MISSED | harmful + undecided |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pqcheck 0.1.0 | 1 | 6 (all ROLE_INCOMPATIBLE) | 0 | 2 | 21 | 6/30 = 20% |
| pqc-audit 0.4.1 | 1 | 0 | 3 | 0 | 26 | 3/30 = 10% |
| PostQuant 0.7.0 | 4 | 3 (all FALSE_ASSURANCE) | 3 | 0 | 20 | 6/30 = 20% |
| qsafe 1.1.0 | 6 | 2 (ROLE_INCOMPATIBLE) | 0 | 0 | 22 | 2/30 = 7% |
| QSA regex stage | 7 | 5 (ROLE_INCOMPATIBLE) | 3 | 0 | 15 | 8/30 = 27% |

Mehl's scanner output for paramiko contains 88 import-level findings (`from cryptography.hazmat.primitives.asymmetric import ec` etc.) classified vulnerable/safe/unknown with no call-site operation and no recommendation; it cannot be judged at site level and is reported as repository-level only.

Concrete failure mechanisms observed (all verifiable in `raw_tool_outputs/`):

- **ML-KEM recommended for signing/verification.** pqcheck labels every pyca RSA finding `asymmetric-encryption` and its policy emits "Shor — migrate to ML-KEM-768" at `rsakey.py:143` (inside `self.key.sign(...)`), `rsakey.py:171` (`key.verify(...)`), `cryptography_backend.py:306` (`sign`) and `:316` (`verify`). QSA's regex does the same at the same sites. These are `ROLE_INCOMPATIBLE`.
- **ML-DSA recommended for ECDH key generation.** pqcheck and qsafe map `ec.generate_private_key` at `kex_ecdh_nist.py:59` to ECDSA → "migrate to ML-DSA-65", although the only consumer is `self.P.exchange(ec.ECDH(), …)`. pqc-audit and PostQuant emit a dual "ML-KEM (ECDH) / ML-DSA (ECDSA)" target (UNDECIDED).
- **False assurance.** PostQuant's `python-pqc-pqcrypto` pattern has call patterns `/sign\s*\(/` and `/encrypt\s*\(/`, so `self.key.sign(...)` (paramiko `rsakey.py:141`), `self.signing_key.sign(...)` (`ecdsakey.py:225`) and the RSA key-wrap `self.prepared_key.encrypt(...)` (`cryptography_backend.py:380`) are reported as "Already using PQC — verify algorithm is NIST-approved".
- **Dual targets on RSA key generation.** pqc-audit, PostQuant and QSA emit "ML-KEM … / ML-DSA …" for `rsa.generate_private_key` (paramiko `rsakey.py:188`), whose key is only ever used to sign.
- **Loaders are invisible.** No tool reports any of the 11 `load_*`/numbers-construction sites except QSA's regex on two of them (dual target). Key provenance is therefore absent from every baseline's reasoning.
- **Hybrid blindness.** pqcheck flags the X25519 half of paramiko's `mlkem768x25519-sha256` exchange as quantum-vulnerable (`kex_mlkem.py:76/:84`) while separately recording ML-KEM as approved; QSA recommends ML-KEM for the X25519 `exchange` inside the hybrid (OVER_RECOMMENDATION). No tool links the two.
- **Parameter constants reported as findings.** pqcheck and QSA report `RSA1_5 = padding.PKCS1v15()` (`cryptography_backend.py:208`) as an RSA finding with an ML-KEM target (detection-level false positive).

### 3.3 Miss rates

Between 50% and 87% of eligible sites receive no finding from a given tool. Most misses are operation calls on attribute receivers (`self.key.sign`), which no AST-based baseline catalogues (pqcheck's catalogue has no instance methods; pqc-audit has no sign/verify/exchange rules), and all key loaders.

## 4. Decision against the kill criterion

| Criterion | Threshold (provisional) | Observed | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name-undeterminable share | < ~10% would trigger redesign | 53% (CI 36–70%) | not triggered |
| Baseline harmful + undecided | < ~10% for all tools would trigger redesign | 7%, 10%, 20%, 20%, 27% | not triggered (four of five tools ≥ 10%) |

**Decision: proceed with N1 + N2 (+ N3).** N2 (provenance-aware role inference with abstention) addresses exactly the two mechanisms observed: key sites that need consumer evidence (16/30) and loaders that need provenance (11/30 sites and 0% baseline detection). The four indeterminate sites justify the abstention outcome as a first-class result rather than a tuning trick.

## 5. Limitations of the pilot (carried into the protocol)

1. n = 30 from two repositories; CIs are wide; both repositories are in baseline tuning corpora (biases *toward* the baselines).
2. Single-library (pyca) sites only; PyCryptodome idioms untested; PK_ENCRYPT/PK_DECRYPT have n = 1 each.
3. Annotator is an AI; the independent review was also an AI in a separate context. Human review is required and is a Phase 1 gate condition.
4. Baseline adapters were written for this pilot and must be published and re-run from packaged code in Phase 2; QSA was run without its LLM stage; CBOMkit and Mehl's scanner were not run.
5. The line-window matching (site range) may credit a tool for a finding on a neighbouring token (e.g. pqcheck's finding on the `padding.PKCS1v15()` argument of a `sign` call is attributed to the `sign` site). This is intentional (the tool's recommendation applies to that line) and is documented in the adapter.
